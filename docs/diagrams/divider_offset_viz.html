Resistive voltage divider — what it does to a signal that has a DC offset

The divider (÷50)

  Vin ──[ R_top = 980k ]──●── Vout = Vin × R_bot/(R_top+R_bot) = Vin × 1/50
                          │
                       [ R_bot = 20k ]
                          │
                         GND        (input impedance ≈ 1 MΩ)

ratio = 20k / (980k + 20k) = 0.02 = ÷50. A divider is linear, so it multiplies the whole waveform — DC offset AND the AC swing — by the same 1/50. The offset is scaled down proportionally; it is not removed.

BEFORE — at the probe tip

250 Vpp sine riding on a +50 V DC offset → swings −75 V … +175 V.

AFTER — past the ÷50 divider

Everything ÷50: 5 Vpp sine on a +1 V offset → swings −1.5 V … +3.5 V.

The key idea

Amplitude (pp)DC offsetMean
Before250 V+50 V+50 V
After ÷505 V+1 V+1 V

The divider shrank the offset from +50 V to +1 V — but the signal is still off-centre by +1 V. So a divider gets you into range, but it cannot centre the trace. Removing the offset is a separate active step — you subtract a matching voltage at the amplifier's summing node (your offset DAC), which is what lets you then zoom the AC part without the residual DC clipping. Divider = scale; offset DAC = centre.

⚠️ 250 Vpp is shown only to illustrate the principle. This instrument is rated ≤30 V, no mains, no isolation — do not feed it real high voltage.
